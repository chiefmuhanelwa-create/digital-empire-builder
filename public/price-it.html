Other things to think about

"Should I charge less to build my name?"

If you discount, show it. Put the full price on the quote, then the discount, then what they pay. The client learns what your work is worth. Next time you charge the full price and nobody is shocked.

Never go below your lowest safe price (the number under your price). Below it, you are paying to work.

Cancellations and late payers
  • Say in the quote that the deposit is not refundable if they cancel inside 7 days. You turned other work away for that date.
  • Moving the date is fine once, if the new date is open.
  • Late balance? Don't send the final files. That's why the previews are watermarked.
Packages instead of one price

Give three options: small, standard and full. Price each one through this calculator. Most people pick the middle one, so make the middle one the job you want to do most.

Phase pricing for bigger jobs

A short film, a music video or a wedding film is never one flat number. Price it in phases: plan, shoot, edit. Each phase is paid before it starts. If the client stops halfway, you have already been paid for what you did. Pick "In phases" in step 6.

When to raise your price

Don't grow because you went viral. Grow when your calendar is full. If step 7 says you need more hours than you can sell, raise the price. Don't work more hours.

Selling the same work again

Make it once. Sell it again and again. Prints of your area, presets, or a phone-photography class for beginners are all ways to earn from work you've already done.

Example numbers only show the method. Use your own hours, costs and rate. Ask a registered tax practitioner in your country about setting money aside for tax.
